## The Short Answer

Rosebud is an empathetic AI journal. Aurelius Agent is a Stoic journal with an AI mentor who holds you to what you said. Both read your entries. They do it from opposite philosophical positions: Rosebud meets you with warmth, Aurelius Agent meets you with honesty.

If you want to feel understood, pick Rosebud. If you want to be confronted, pick Aurelius Agent. Each is the wrong choice for the other's user: supportive feedback fails the person who needs structural honesty, and structural honesty fails the person who is already in the deep end emotionally.

## What Rosebud Is

Rosebud has earned its place in AI journaling by leaning into *empathy*. Its dig-deeper questions are meant to feel supportive. The AI plays the role of a thoughtful friend who notices what you are not saying and asks gentle follow-ups.

Strengths:

- Emotional intelligence in tone. The follow-ups feel human.
- Questions that build on what you have shared, so you keep exploring instead of starting over.
- A good fit for people in transition (grief, breakup, career shift) who need a non-judgmental space to process.

Limits:

- The tone is, by design, supportive. It does not confront drift.
- Its insight is about themes and feelings, not about whether you kept what you promised.
- Accountability is whatever you ask for. The default is empathy.

## What Aurelius Agent Is

Aurelius Agent starts from the opposite assumption: that what many people need from a journal is the truth told back to them, on a steady rhythm, whether they want to hear it or not.

The loop:

1. **Morning Account.** What is on your mind, what waits, what could go wrong. Type it, or tap the microphone to dictate and edit before saving.
2. **What Must Be Done.** An optional section (off by default; add it in Journal Sections): the one to three things the day fails without. With its reminder bell on, an evening message in chat asks about each, and you mark it Kept or Missed.
3. **Evening Examination.** What you did well, what badly, what you left undone. After you save a morning or evening entry, he answers.
4. **The Measure.** A separate 30-second daily score: Energy, Focus, Body and Satisfaction, each 1 to 10, plus one optional thing to mend.
5. **Weekly Lesson.** He reads your week: an AURA Level from 0 to 100, your profile, the story of your week, and one challenge you accept and sign. The next Lesson asks whether you met it. During The 30-Day Protocol it lands on day 6, then about every seven days. After that, Sunday at 19:00 your time.

Over time, when a habit shows in your entries on two separate days at least a week apart, it becomes a constellation in your Archive. Seen on five separate days, it is proven. Chat remembers your record across sessions, and What Aurelius Knows in Settings lists the facts he holds, any of which you can remove.

The voice is honest before kind. Not hostile: there is no hostility in Stoicism, only clarity. The app does not exist to make you feel better. It exists to make you *more accurate* about how you are living.

## When to Pick Rosebud

- You are processing something heavy and need a journal that meets you where you are.
- You want empathetic follow-up questions after you write.
- The thing you need is to *feel heard* before you can examine your patterns.
- You are still figuring out what you think and want a tool that helps you discover it gently.

## When to Pick Aurelius Agent

- You already know what you think. Your problem is doing it.
- You have a history of starting and abandoning journaling apps because the feedback was too soft to keep you engaged.
- You want to be asked each evening about what you said you would do, and to mark it Kept or Missed, without having to start the conversation yourself.
- You suspect that the missing ingredient is honest feedback, not better questions.
- You want the *practice* of the Stoic evening review, not just the aesthetic.

## The Real Question

Both of these apps work. The choice is not about quality. It is about *which feedback shape you respond to*.

Some people get more honest with themselves when they feel safe. Others get more honest with themselves when they are confronted. Many people lean one way or the other, and the wrong tool wastes months.

Be honest about which one you are. Then pick the tool that matches.
