You name what must be done and mark it Kept or Missed at night. An AI mentor reads what you wrote, answers, names the habits he sees returning, and writes you a Weekly Lesson with one challenge.

**Who it is for:** people who already read the Stoics and want to live it daily, not collect more quotes.

## What do most Stoic apps actually do?

Most apps in the category fall into one of three groups:

- **Quote apps.** A line from Marcus Aurelius, Seneca or Epictetus each morning, sometimes with a widget. Good for reading. Nothing to do.
- **Wellness journals with a Stoic theme.** Mood check-ins, breathing exercises, prompts. Useful tools, but they ask how you feel, where the Stoics asked how you acted.
- **AI Marcus chatbots.** You talk to a character. The conversation can be good, but some remember little of your actual days.

None of that is wrong. It just is not the practice the Stoics wrote about.

## What does a real Stoic practice need?

Three things, all from the sources:

1. **Prepare in the morning.** Marcus opens Book 2 of the Meditations by telling himself that today he will meet meddling, ungrateful and arrogant people, so he is ready for them. Decide who you will be before the day decides for you.
2. **Examine the evening.** Seneca (On Anger 3.36) went over his whole day each night, hiding nothing from himself. What did I do well, what badly, what did I leave undone?
3. **Watch what repeats.** The Meditations return to the same faults again and again. The practice is noticing which correction keeps coming back.

## How does Aurelius Agent do each of those?

**Morning.** The Morning Account: what is on your mind, what waits, what could go wrong. Add The Pre-Mortem if you want the old premeditation in full: how tomorrow breaks, and the move that stops it. The next day it asks how it went.

**Evening.** The Evening Examination asks what you did well, what badly and what you left undone. Aurelius answers each entry. If you named what must be done that morning, he asks about each item in the evening and you mark it Kept or Missed.

**What repeats.** When a habit shows up in your entries on separate days, he names it and shows you the days it came from. Each one becomes a constellation in your Archive, named by what sets it off. Seen on five separate days, it is marked proven.

**The week.** The Weekly Lesson reads your week and ends with one challenge for the next. You sign it, and the next Lesson asks whether you met it.

**When your mind is crowded.** Practices such as The Sort (what is up to you and what is not) and Impression (what happened, and what you told yourself about it).

## What it does not do

It is iPhone only. There is no free tier, no Android or web version, no data export and no community. There is a streak counter and a rank that only rises, but nothing marks a day as failed. It is a journal and a mentor, not therapy.

## Is a notebook enough?

For the practice itself, yes. Plenty of people keep it in a notebook. The app is for people whose notebook went quiet after a week, or who want a reader who compares tonight with this morning and remembers last month.
